02 / The method

How ASHS turns scans into region labels

ASHS aligns labeled atlas examples to a new subject and combines their proposed labels. The sequence below summarizes the main operations, including the second pass that refines the initial result.

  1. 01T1 + T2 MRIComplementary anatomical views
  2. 02Initial alignmentPlace images in correspondence
  3. 03Rough segmentationBuild the initial subject labels
  4. 04Affine refinementStage 4 · ml_affine
  5. 05Nonlinear registrationRefine local correspondence
  6. 06Label propagationCarry atlas labels into subject space
  7. 07Joint label fusionCombine the atlas proposals
  8. 08Corrective learningApply configured learned corrections
  9. 09Final segmentationRegion labels and volume outputs
Atlas & registration

An atlas is a collection of labeled reference MRI examples and supporting resources. Registration mathematically aligns anatomical images.

Affine & nonlinear alignment

An affine transformation applies global translation, rotation, scaling and shear. Nonlinear registration allows local deformable alignment.

Propagation & fusion

Label propagation transfers atlas labels through a registration into the target subject. Joint label fusion combines proposals from multiple registered examples.

04 / Forensic investigation

The divergence begins with the affine fit

For left/train000, the saved rough subject labels entering Stage 4 were identical.

0 differing voxels1.0 Dice

The first saved differing output in this comparison was bs_affine.mat.

ml_affine samples points on segmentation label surfaces and fits a global affine transformation between the subject’s rough labels and an atlas example. The resulting 4 × 4 matrix affects later registration.

Translation, rotation, scaling and shear provide the global fit. Later nonlinear registration refines local alignment.

Online first RMSD also equals 2.9808. RMSD means Root Mean Square Distance, a surface-point fitting diagnostic. A lower value means a closer fit according to that calculation. It does not establish anatomical accuracy, and no physical unit is assumed here.

Surface sampling was one identified implementation difference. Libraries and other build details also differed. A controlled experiment would be needed to isolate sampling as the sole cause.

09 / Current research phase

MRI motion degradation

Planned / current work

Motion during scanning can reduce image quality, making it a concern for pediatric MRI. The next experiment will use TorchIO to simulate motion on clean MRI and study the effect on ASHS labels.

  1. Clean MRI
  2. TorchIO motion simulation
  3. Degraded MRI
  4. ASHS segmentation
  5. Compare with clean baseline

A controlled image-quality comparison

Record the simulation settings and random seed. Compare segmentation changes across motion conditions while keeping the ASHS setup and evaluation rules fixed.

Candidate measures include Dice overlap, surface distances and voxel-label mismatch. Agreement with the clean ASHS baseline measures stability. Expert/manual labels are needed to assess anatomical accuracy.

10 / Literature review in progress

Evolution of MRI motion correction

A working outline for the literature review. These overlapping method families will be populated with verified papers and dates as the review develops.

  1. Early conventional approaches
  2. Prospective & retrospective correction
  3. Registration-based methods
  4. Reconstruction-based methods
  5. Learning-based methods
  6. Deep learning / AI methods

Long-term direction

Determine whether image-quality improvement can recover or improve segmentation reliability in motion-degraded pediatric MRI. Super-resolution, including ECLARE, remains part of this direction. Improving degraded scans could potentially increase usable study data. The hippocampus is the initial focus; other structures may follow.

No improvement in segmentation accuracy from ECLARE or motion correction has been established by this project.
